What is H3?

The short version, so you know what you are prompting.

One name, two names

MiniMax H3 is the official name. Hailuo 3.0 and Hailuo 03 are the same model, named after the Hailuo app. It follows Hailuo 2.3.

Omni-modal input

It takes text, images, video and audio in one model. That means you can prompt from words alone or steer it with references.

Video plus sound

One generation returns the picture and a stereo audio track together, so you can direct the sound in the same prompt.

Multi-shot aware

H3 plans sequences with several shots. A prompt with time-coded beats gives it a clear plan to follow.

This guide follows community and provider write-ups published after the late-July 2026 launch. Length, resolution and price differ between providers, so check the official MiniMax docs or the platform you use for exact limits.

Pick your mode

  
What you write changes with what you attach.

  

    
Text only
Text to video
Nothing is fixed yet, so describe the opening composition, then the action in order, then the ending state.
Put the camera and the sound in the prompt too. If you leave them out, H3 decides.

    
1 image
Image to video
The image already sets the subject, light and palette. Do not describe the picture again.
Spend your words on what moves, how it moves, and what the camera does.

    
2 images
First and last frame
Start with a line that says how the frames line up, then a blank line, then the prompt.
Describe the path between them: the subject's movement, the camera travel and the final composition. One continuous shot usually works best.

    
Image, video, audio
Reference mode
Name each asset (Image 1, Video 1, Audio 1) and give it one job.
Say what to copy and what to ignore. If two references fight over the same thing, such as lighting, say which one wins.

  






  
Seven rules

  
The habits that separate a lucky clip from a repeatable one.

  

    
Do
    
      
  • Prompt the timeline. Time-code the beats in order and state how the shot ends.
    • 
      
  • Give every reference a role. "Video 1 gives the hand movement only."
    • 
      
  • Say what must stay the same. Face, outfit, product shape, background.
    • 
      
  • Use one camera move per shot, with its speed and size.
    • 
      
  • Use film words. Dolly in, track left, rack focus, handheld, locked-off, whip pan.
    • 
      
  • Direct sound as layers. Dialogue, physical sounds, ambience and music, each on its own line.
    • 
      
  • Finish with limits. "No text, no logos, no extra people."
    • 
    

    
Don't
    
      
  • Pile on adjectives. "Epic, stunning, cinematic" tells the model very little.
    • 
      
  • Redescribe the start image. It is already in the frame.
    • 
      
  • Chain several camera moves in one shot unless you want cuts. They may be split into separate shots.
    • 
      
  • Leave sound blank if it matters. The mix is left to chance.
    • 
      
  • Say "use the reference" without saying which part. Name what transfers and what does not.
    • 
      
  • Cram 15 seconds with 10 actions. Fewer, clearer beats read better.
    •
